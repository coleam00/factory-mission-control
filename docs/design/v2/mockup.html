What V2 changes

Mockup for discussion. The map is authored by hand and checked against the pinned workflows, so it stays simple and never silently goes stale.

Three screens instead of eleven

  • Factory: the map, with where all the work is right now.
  • Board: work by who holds it, under its work order, each with its round history.
  • Decisions: what the manager brought you, with its recommended answer.
  • Cut: scorecard, versions, diff, decisions, testing, health, rewind, laps. Outcome numbers come from Archon's own metrics.

One line at the top

  • Three ways in (product document, new issue, found by the factory), one Intake, a work order, then one line: Build, Review, App check, Merge, Staging.
  • Build is one card. It opens in three levels: rounds, then delivery rounds, then the review lenses.
  • Plumbing (claims, facts, policy, report, prune) is never drawn. It is named in each step's "behind the scenes".

Work and work orders (Cole and Rasmus, 11/04)

  • Work is any single item the factory takes. A work order is a group of work that goes through together.
  • A product document is one source of work, not the unit of work. Split is a step off Intake, not the front door.
  • Holdouts are gone: they doubled the time per PR and found what review and CI already found. Review's lenses and the App check are the validation.

Hand-authored, machine-checked

  • One factory-map.json: views, positions, captions, copy, and which Archon workflow and node ids each card covers.
  • A check fails when the pinned pack has a workflow or node no card covers, or a card names one that is gone.
  • Live counts attach through those node ids, so the drawing is yours and the numbers are Archon's.

Matches the new design

  • The unit is the work item (Archon's work/<n> state), not the lap.
  • App check sits on the line between Review and Merge: Merge will not land a PR until its result is green, and that result stands until the PR gets a new commit.
  • Owner questions are Archon attention items, not a GitHub issue.

Color means who, not how worried to be

  • Sky: where work comes from, and scripts. Blue: agents building. Green: testing and shipping.
  • Violet: you and the manager. A question is a normal part of the process, so it is never drawn as a warning.
  • Red is kept for real failures only. Amber is not used. Nothing is grey.
